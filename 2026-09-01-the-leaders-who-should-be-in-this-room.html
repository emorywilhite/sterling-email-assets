SEPTEMBER 2026

 

Take the mirror test

Every organization has two versions of itself.

There is the one leaders believe they are running. And there is the one employees and customers live in every day.

Most of the time, nobody puts the two side by side.

The Mirror Test does. It asks one question: Would an independent team see the same organization you see?

The mirror is not a survey. It is not a score. It is not an afternoon of reflection. It is a trained outside team comparing what your organization says should happen with what people actually do, what customers actually get, and what the results can prove.

There are two ways to use it this month. You can learn to hold the mirror. Or you can invite it in.

 

THE BLIND SPOT

 

You cannot see your own organization from the inside

Not all of it. I cannot see all of mine either.

You know the plan. You know what you asked people to do. What you cannot see is the space between that and what happens on a Tuesday afternoon in a department you do not sit in.

That space is normal. Every organization has one. The trouble starts when the people closest to it stop noticing it is there.

Finding it is what Sterling Examiners are trained to do.

An Examiner compares what an organization says should happen with what its people do, what its customers experience, and what its results show. The team writes down what they found. What is working. What is not. What to fix first.

You may think that work belongs to one kind of job.

It does not.

Sterling Examiners come from healthcare, education, government, manufacturing, nonprofits, and businesses of every size. Some work in quality or improvement. Others lead people, run operations, study data, serve customers, or build strategy.

The title on the door is not what matters. Judgment is.

If a name just came to mind, forward them this email. The best person for the role is often waiting for someone else to say it first.

Here is what the work looks like. Sterling teaches you the Framework for Performance Excellence, all seven areas, from Leadership to Results. Then you use it on a real organization, next to people from other fields who notice what you miss.

You see what a strong system looks like from the inside.

You go back to your own team and start asking the same questions.
