Five problems. Or one?

Good morning,

The communication problem.

The stalled initiative.

The goal that keeps slipping.

The process everyone quietly works around.

The issue your team fixed six months ago that is somehow back again.

They look like five different problems.

They may not be.

And that is where even very capable leaders can get stuck.

When you work inside an organization every day, you get close enough to solve what is directly in front of you. Sometimes, you get too close to see what is connecting it all.

Most leadership development teaches you how to make better decisions.

Sterling Examiner training teaches you how to see the system those decisions live inside.

As an Examiner, you apply the Baldrige Excellence Framework to a real organization. You learn to look past the obvious symptoms, test your assumptions, and identify the gaps influencing performance.

Then you bring that way of seeing back to your own organization.

Kyle Jordan puts it plainly:

“Serving as an Examiner has been hands-down the most effective leadership development accelerator, and leads to better outcomes for my team and organization.”

Kyle Jordan, MBA, MSN, RN, TCTCN, CPHQ, Sterling Examiner

You do not develop that perspective alone. You work with leaders from different fields whose questions, experiences, and points of view can expose what one person might miss.

“As a Team Leader, I work with a team of Examiners with a wide range of experience and perspectives who challenge my own personal biases and help me grow. It's a gift to learn and network with other high-performing organizations.”

Jeff Cooper, Chief Operating Officer, Hemophilia of Georgia, 2025 Master Examiner
